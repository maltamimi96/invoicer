You write the first line. Nothing else in the post matters if this fails.

You get roughly one second. Not one sentence — one second.

## What works

- **A specific number.** "$400 now or $9,000 in March" beats "roof repairs can
  get expensive."
- **A wrong belief, named.** "Your roof doesn't leak where the stain is."
- **The thing they've already noticed.** "That brown ring on your ceiling has
  been there a month, hasn't it."
- **A stake.** "Whoever quoted you $1,200 for this hasn't been in the roof."

## What doesn't

- Warm-up. "In this video I'm going to explain…" — they're gone.
- Vagueness. "Roof maintenance is important." Nobody has ever stopped for this.
- Clickbait the post can't pay off. If the hook promises a number, the post owes
  a number. A hook that lies costs more than a hook that's dull, because it
  burns the account.
- Anything the client can't stand behind.

## Rules

- Under about 12 words. Say it out loud — if you run out of breath, cut it.
- Lead with the concrete noun. Not "many homeowners find that…" — "That stain."
- Write it in *their* voice, not ad-copy voice. A tradie who talks like a
  brochure reads as a fake tradie.
- One hook per angle, and it must be the hook for **that** angle. If a hook
  would work equally well on another angle, it's too generic.

Give a couple of alternates per angle so there's something to pick from — but
make the first one your actual recommendation, not the safest.
